I know I usually post tech content and guides on this blog, but I've been thinking of late of posting more personal thoughts on here, too, just as a way to get them out of my own head and better understand my own position on certain things.

It's an experiment, and I write this blog for fun, so we'll see how it goes.

This particular post is inspired by all the controversy the last few years in regards technology and its impact on our lives with regards to personal privacy.

Most people don't think about all the microphones and cameras that are now surrounding them 24/7, but it's something that everyone really needs to be aware of. The phone in your pocket, the smart speaker on your kitchen island - you really have no control over what they record and when, unless you're willing to do a little surgery with a soldering iron.

That's also ignoring the multitude of tracking that's built into the software we use daily, including the very operating systems that run our devices.

They claim that all of this data is 'anonymized', and it may well be - but once you have enough data points, it's really not hard to de-anonymize that same data. See the recent news about how the ['Scatter Spider' hacker got caught.](https://darkatlas.io/blog/from-windows-telemetry-to-arrest-how-microsofts-gdid-helped-connect-the-dots).

So many people, even people that I know in my personal life, have the common attitude of ["I have nothing to hide"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing_to_hide_argument).

I absolutely _loathe_ this argument.

Someone wanting to keep their private life private shouldn't even be a question. There are a myriad of reasons I might not want anyone to know every intimate detail of my life.

I am not arguing that someone like the above alleged hacker shouldn't be caught - far from it. But I have serious problems with their methodology.

In order for Microsoft to provide this information to authorities, it means that they are tracking this same data on _every single device_ running Windows. At _minimum_ Windows 11 is doing this, but I've read reports that this GDID recording goes back to Windows 10 and even possibly before.

And they are far from the only ones. Facebook, Google, Amazon - all of these companies are casting these incredibly wide nets of data collection for dubious reasons. Targeted advertising is honestly the most palatable of them.

Just a few other examples of how this data can be misused:

- [Targeting dissents](https://www.404media.co/ice-cbp-secret-service-all-broke-law-with-smartphone-location-data/)
- [Election manipulation](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/sep/10/facebook-cambridge-analytica-new-mexico-trial)
- [Dynamic pricing](https://apnews.com/article/ftc-federal-trade-commission-personalized-prices-surveillance-daa65642d74dc7521da0ab010820f373)

When it comes right down to it, it's about _power,_ and who has it.

Writing a post like this feels a bit like shouting into the wind.

The people who know already know, and it's difficult to make the non-technical people in our lives understand or care.

Running alternative operating systems like Linux and GrapheneOS can help up to a point, but that doesn't help you avoid things like [browser fingerprinting](https://nordlayer.com/learn/browser-security/browser-fingerprinting/) and honestly in some cases it makes it worse, because the number of people using these tools is so low by comparison to the people running stock Android / iOS / Windows.

But you _should_ care about your privacy, because no one else is going to.
